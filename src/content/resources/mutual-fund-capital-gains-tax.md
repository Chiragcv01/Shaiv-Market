Tax on mutual fund gains depends on two things: **how long you held the units**, and **which tax group the scheme falls in**. Every scheme belongs to one of three groups, and the holding period and rates follow from that group. The first table explains the groups; the next ones show where each AMFI scheme category sits. To work out the tax on your own redemption, use the [mutual fund tax calculator](/calculators/mutual-fund-tax-calculator).

## 1. The three tax groups

| Fund type | What qualifies | Long term after | STCG rate | LTCG rate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Equity-oriented funds** | At least 65% invested in listed Indian equity shares | 12 months | 20% (Sec 111A) | 12.5% on gains above ₹1.25 lakh per financial year (Sec 112A) |
| **Debt-oriented funds** | More than 65% in debt and money-market instruments, or a fund of funds investing 65% or more in such funds | Never: always short term (Sec 50AA) | Slab rate | Slab rate (same as STCG) |
| **Other funds** | Funds in neither group, such as hybrids with 35-65% equity, gold and overseas funds | 24 months (listed ETFs: 12 months) | Slab rate | 12.5%, no indexation, no ₹1.25 lakh exemption |

## 3. Specialised Investment Funds (SIF)

SIFs are launched by mutual fund houses under SEBI's mutual fund framework, with a minimum investment of ₹10 lakh per fund house. The fund itself pays no tax on its trades; you are taxed on redemption on the same basis as mutual funds, depending on the scheme's equity or debt exposure.

| SIF category | Sub-categories | Tax group | STCG | LTCG |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Equity-oriented SIFs | Equity Long-Short; Equity Ex-Top 100 Long-Short; Sector Rotation Long-Short | Equity-oriented | 20% (within 12 months) | 12.5% above ₹1.25 lakh per financial year (after 12 months) |
| Hybrid SIFs | Hybrid Long-Short; Active Asset Allocator Long-Short | By actual equity % (arbitrage counts as equity): 65% or more is equity-oriented, 35-65% is other funds, over 65% debt is debt-oriented | 20% if equity-oriented, else slab rate | 12.5% above ₹1.25 lakh if equity-oriented; 12.5% after 24 months with no exemption if other funds; slab rate if debt-oriented |
| Debt-oriented SIFs | Debt Long-Short; Sectoral Debt Long-Short | Debt-oriented | Slab rate | Slab rate (no LTCG) |

A hybrid SIF's tax treatment depends on its actual portfolio; confirm it in the scheme's investment strategy information document (ISID).

## 4. Key points to remember

- **₹1.25 lakh exemption.** It applies once per financial year to the total LTCG from all equity-oriented funds and listed shares combined, not per scheme.
- **Debt units bought before 1 April 2023.** These can still become long term after 24 months, taxed at 12.5% without indexation. Short-term gains remain at your slab rate.
- **Surcharge and cess.** All rates above exclude surcharge (where applicable) and the 4% health and education cess.
- **Losses.** Short-term losses can be set off against both short-term and long-term gains; long-term losses only against long-term gains. Both can be carried forward for 8 years if you file your return on time.
- **Dividends (IDCW).** Taxed as income at your slab rate, separate from capital gains.
- **Holding period.** Counted unit by unit, first in first out, from the allotment date of each SIP instalment.

## Questions people ask

### How is tax calculated on equity mutual funds?
Gains on units held 12 months or less are short-term and taxed at 20%. Gains on units held more than 12 months are long-term and taxed at 12.5%, but only on the part above ₹1.25 lakh of total equity long-term gains in the financial year. 4% cess applies on top.

### How are debt mutual funds taxed now?
Debt-oriented funds bought on or after 1 April 2023 are always taxed at your income tax slab rate, however long you hold them (Section 50AA). Units bought before 1 April 2023 become long-term after 24 months and are then taxed at 12.5% without indexation.

### How are hybrid funds taxed?
It depends on the fund's equity share. Funds with 65% or more in Indian equity (including arbitrage) are taxed like equity funds. Funds with 35-65% equity are "other funds": slab rate within 24 months, 12.5% without the ₹1.25 lakh exemption after that. Funds with less than 35% equity are taxed like debt funds.

### How are gold ETFs and gold fund of funds taxed?
Listed gold and silver ETFs are long-term after 12 months and taxed at 12.5%, with no ₹1.25 lakh exemption; within 12 months, at your slab rate. Gold and silver fund of funds need 24 months for the 12.5% rate.

### Is the ₹1.25 lakh LTCG exemption per fund?
No. It is one limit per financial year across all your long-term gains from equity-oriented mutual funds and listed shares combined.

### How is the holding period counted for a SIP?
Each SIP instalment has its own holding period, counted from its allotment date. When you redeem, units are sold first in, first out, so older instalments may be long-term while recent ones are still short-term.

### Are IDCW (dividend) payouts taxed as capital gains?
No. IDCW payouts are added to your income and taxed at your slab rate. Capital gains tax applies only when you redeem or switch units.

### Is switching between schemes taxable?
Yes. A switch from one scheme to another, or between plans of the same scheme, is treated as a redemption and can trigger capital gains tax.
