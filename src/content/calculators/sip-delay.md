## What this calculator shows

Most people delay starting a SIP for sensible-sounding reasons: waiting for the market to fall, for the next increment, for the wedding to be over. This calculator puts a rupee figure on that wait.

Both scenarios end on the **same date**, because your goal date doesn't move just because you started late. The only difference is how many years your money gets to compound.

## How to use it

1. **Monthly SIP** you plan to invest.
2. **Years until you need the money**, counted from today.
3. **If you wait for**: the delay you are considering.
4. **Expected return**.

You get the cost of waiting, and the bigger SIP you would need after the delay to end up in the same place.

## Worked example: ₹10,000 a month, 25-year goal, 12%

| You start | Value at the goal date | Cost of waiting | SIP needed to catch up |
|---|---|---|---|
| Today | ₹1.90 crore | — | ₹10,000 |
| 1 year late | ₹1.67 crore | ₹22.5 lakh | ₹11,345 |
| 2 years late | ₹1.47 crore | ₹42.5 lakh | ₹12,882 |
| 3 years late | ₹1.30 crore | ₹60.2 lakh | ₹14,643 |
| 5 years late | ₹1.00 crore | ₹89.9 lakh | ₹18,993 |
| 10 years late | ₹50.0 lakh | ₹1.39 crore | ₹37,609 |

A one-year delay means skipping ₹1.2 lakh of instalments and losing ₹22.5 lakh at the end. The lost money is not the instalments you skipped; it is the final years of compounding on everything else, which are the most valuable years of all.

## "But what if the market falls after I start?"

Then your SIP buys more units at lower prices, which helps when markets recover. A SIP is built for exactly that situation. Waiting for a "better time" usually means waiting while the clock runs, and very few people manage to time their entry well. The [SIP calculator](/calculators/sip-calculator) shows what you build if you start now; the paperless KYC to start takes about ten minutes.

## Questions people ask

### What is the cost of delay in SIP?
It is the difference between what a SIP would grow to if you started today and what it grows to if you started later, with the same end date. At 12% over 25 years, a one-year delay on a ₹10,000 SIP costs about ₹22.5 lakh.

### Is it better to start a small SIP now or a big one later?
Usually a small SIP now. Every year of compounding you add at the start increases the final value more than a larger amount added later. You can raise the SIP each year with a step-up.

### Should I wait for the market to fall before starting a SIP?
Timing the market consistently is very hard. A SIP spreads your purchases over many months, so it does not depend on getting the start date right.

### How much more do I need to invest if I start late?
In the example above, starting five years late needs ₹18,993 a month instead of ₹10,000, nearly double, to reach the same amount on the same date.

### Does the cost of delay apply to lumpsum investments too?
Yes. A lumpsum invested a year later also loses a year of compounding. The effect is the same, just easier to see with SIPs.
