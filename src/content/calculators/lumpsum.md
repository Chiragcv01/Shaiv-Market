## How to use the lumpsum calculator

1. **One-time investment**: the amount you are investing in one go, such as a bonus, an FD that matured, or money from a property sale.
2. **Expected return** and **period**.
3. **Inflation** (optional): used only to show the result in today's money, so you can see what it will really buy.

## The lumpsum formula

**Future value = P × (1 + r)<sup>n</sup>**

where *P* is the amount, *r* the yearly return and *n* the number of years. The money compounds once a year in this calculator.

A quick mental check is the **rule of 72**: divide 72 by the return to get the years it takes to double. At 12% that is 6 years (the exact answer is 6.1). At 8% it is 9 years.

## Worked example: ₹5 lakh at 12%

The last column is the sobering one. Your ₹5 lakh still grows a lot in real terms, but less than the headline number suggests. Plan your goals on the right-hand column.

## Lumpsum, SIP or STP?

Putting a large amount into equity on one day means your result depends a lot on that day's market level. Two common ways to soften that:

- **STP (systematic transfer plan)**: park the lumpsum in a liquid or short-term debt fund and transfer a fixed amount into an equity fund every week or month, over 6 to 12 months.
- **Split by goal**: money needed within three years should not go into equity at all, lumpsum or otherwise.

Over long periods, investing a lumpsum straight away has more often ended ahead simply because the money is invested for longer. An STP buys peace of mind, and that matters if a fall right after investing would make you sell. If the money will come in monthly instead, use the [SIP calculator](/calculators/sip-calculator).

## Questions people ask

### How much will ₹1 lakh become in 10 years?
About ₹3.1 lakh at 12% a year, ₹2.6 lakh at 10% and ₹2.2 lakh at 8%. Returns from market-linked funds are not guaranteed.

### Is lumpsum better than SIP?
For the same amount available today, a lumpsum is invested longer, so it can do better in a rising market. A SIP or STP spreads the entry points and reduces the risk of investing everything just before a fall. Which suits you depends on how you would react to an early loss.

### What is the minimum lumpsum investment in a mutual fund?
Many schemes accept a one-time investment from ₹100 to ₹5,000, depending on the scheme. The scheme document lists the minimum.

### How is the lumpsum return calculated?
With compound interest: amount × (1 + yearly return) raised to the number of years. The calculator compounds once a year.

### Is the lumpsum return taxable?
Only when you sell. Gains on equity funds held over 12 months are taxed at 12.5% above ₹1.25 lakh a year. The [mutual fund tax calculator](/calculators/mutual-fund-tax-calculator) works it out.
