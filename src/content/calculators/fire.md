## How to use the FIRE calculator

1. **Current age** and **target FIRE age**.
2. **Monthly expenses** today and **monthly income**.
3. **Monthly investment** you make now, and **current savings / net worth** you would use.
4. **Inflation** and **expected return**.

It shows your FIRE number, along with a lean version (about 75% of it, for a frugal lifestyle) and a fat one (about 135%, for a comfortable one). It also tells you whether your current savings rate gets you there and, if not, the monthly investment that would.

## How the FIRE number is worked out

1. Today's yearly expenses are inflated to your FIRE age.
2. That yearly figure is divided by a **safe withdrawal rate** (SWR). The calculator uses 3.5%.

FIRE corpus = yearly expenses at FIRE age ÷ 3.5%, which is about **28.6 times** your yearly expenses at that age.

## Why 3.5% and not the famous 4% rule

The 4% rule comes from US studies of 30-year retirements. Retiring at 45 in India is different: your money may need to last 40 years or more, and Indian inflation has historically run higher than American inflation. A lower withdrawal rate leaves more room for bad years.

| Withdrawal rate | Corpus as a multiple of yearly expenses |
|---|---|
| 4% | 25 times |
| 3.5% | about 28.6 times |
| 3% | about 33 times |

## Worked example

Age 30, expenses ₹60,000 a month, FIRE at 45, ₹15 lakh already invested, 6% inflation, 13% expected return.

| Item | Figure |
|---|---|
| Expenses at 45 | ₹17.3 lakh a year (₹1.44 lakh a month) |
| FIRE corpus at 3.5% | ₹4.93 crore |
| Lean FIRE / Fat FIRE | ₹3.70 crore / ₹6.66 crore |
| ₹15 lakh grows to | about ₹94 lakh |
| Monthly investment needed | about ₹71,800 |

That is a big monthly number, and it should be. FIRE in 15 years usually needs a savings rate of 40 to 60% of take-home income. If that's not realistic, push the FIRE age out a few years or lower expenses; both move the number far more than chasing a higher return.

## Before you quit

- Keep a separate [emergency fund](/calculators/emergency-fund-calculator) on top of the FIRE corpus.
- Buy your own health insurance while you are still employed and healthy.
- Plan for a few big costs the expense figure misses: children's education, a car replacement, home repairs.
- Hold a few years of expenses in debt funds so a market crash doesn't force you to sell equity.

## Questions people ask

### What is FIRE?
FIRE stands for Financial Independence, Retire Early: building a corpus large enough that its returns cover your living costs, so work becomes optional.

### How big a corpus do I need to retire early in India?
A common rule of thumb is 30 to 33 times your yearly expenses, which assumes withdrawing about 3 to 3.5% a year. The calculator adjusts for inflation and your timeline.

### What is lean FIRE and fat FIRE?
Lean FIRE is financial independence on a frugal budget; fat FIRE is on a comfortable, higher budget. The calculator shows both at roughly 75% and 135% of the standard corpus.

### Can I reach FIRE with mutual fund SIPs?
Yes, diversified equity mutual funds are a common core for FIRE plans because of their long-term growth potential and liquidity. Returns are not guaranteed, so plan with a cautious return and review every year.

### What is a safe withdrawal rate for India?
There is no official figure. Many Indian planners use 3 to 3.5% for early retirement because of the longer time horizon and higher inflation, compared with the 4% often quoted for US retirees.
