## What a step-up SIP is

Also called a top-up SIP, a progressive SIP, a growing SIP or an increment SIP: they all mean the same thing. You choose a starting amount and a yearly increase, say 10%. In year one you invest ₹10,000 a month, in year two ₹11,000, in year three ₹12,100, and so on. Most fund houses let you set this up once so it happens automatically on the SIP's anniversary.

It matches how most salaries behave. You commit to an amount that is comfortable today and let it grow with your income, instead of waiting until you "can afford" the bigger number.

## How to use the calculator

1. **Starting monthly SIP**: what you can invest comfortably now.
2. **Step up by a percentage or a fixed ₹ amount**: 5 to 10% a year is realistic for most salaried investors. If you prefer round numbers, choose a fixed amount such as ₹1,000 a year.
3. **Assumed return** and **period**.
4. **Lumpsum (optional)**: a bonus or savings you invest on day one alongside the SIP.
5. **Inflation**: to see what the final amount is worth in today's money.

The chart compares your step-up SIP with a flat SIP of the same starting amount. The table shows the SIP amount for every year, so you can see what you are signing up for in year 15.

## Worked example: ₹10,000 for 20 years at 12%

| Yearly step-up | You invest in total | Value after 20 years | SIP in year 20 |
|---|---|---|---|
| None (flat) | ₹24.0 lakh | ₹99.9 lakh | ₹10,000 |
| 5% | ₹39.7 lakh | ₹1.37 crore | ₹25,270 |
| 10% | ₹68.7 lakh | ₹1.99 crore | ₹61,159 |
| 15% | ₹1.23 crore | ₹3.03 crore | ₹1,42,318 |

The 10% row doubles the end result. Be honest with yourself about the last column, though. A 15% step-up means investing ₹1.4 lakh a month in year 20. Choose a rate your income can actually keep up with.

## Step up by a fixed amount instead of a percentage

Some people find "add ₹1,000 a year" easier than "add 10% a year". A fixed amount grows more slowly than a percentage over long periods:

| ₹10,000 a month, 20 years, assumed 12% | You invest | Value after 20 years | SIP in year 20 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flat SIP | ₹24.0 lakh | ₹99.9 lakh | ₹10,000 |
| + ₹1,000 every year | ₹46.8 lakh | ₹1.58 crore | ₹29,000 |
| + 10% every year | ₹68.7 lakh | ₹1.99 crore | ₹61,159 |

## Step-up SIP with a lumpsum

Adding a one-time ₹1 lakh on day one to the 10% step-up example takes the 20-year value from about ₹1.99 crore to about ₹2.10 crore at an assumed 12%. A lumpsum works hardest when it goes in early.

## Step-up SIP with inflation

₹1.99 crore in 20 years is not ₹1.99 crore of today's buying power. At 6% inflation it buys what about **₹62 lakh** buys today. That is still far more than a flat SIP's ₹99.9 lakh, which is about ₹31 lakh in today's money. A step-up is one of the simplest ways to keep a SIP growing faster than prices. The [inflation calculator](/calculators/inflation-calculator) shows the same for any amount.

## Step-up or a bigger SIP from day one?

If you can afford ₹20,000 today, start at ₹20,000. Money invested earlier always compounds longer. A step-up is for when the bigger number isn't possible yet. The [target SIP calculator](/calculators/required-sip-calculator) shows how much lower the starting SIP can be for the same goal: for ₹1 crore in 15 years at 12%, a flat SIP is ₹19,819, while with a 10% step-up you start at ₹11,516.

## Step-up SIP formula

Each month's instalment compounds at the monthly rate r = yearly return ÷ 12 from the day it is invested. The SIP for year k is:

- **Percentage step-up:** SIP<sub>k</sub> = SIP<sub>1</sub> × (1 + step-up %)<sup>k − 1</sup>
- **Fixed amount step-up:** SIP<sub>k</sub> = SIP<sub>1</sub> + (k − 1) × step-up amount

Each year's 12 instalments grow like a normal SIP for the months left: a year's value at the end of that year is SIP<sub>k</sub> × [((1 + r)<sup>12</sup> − 1) ÷ r] × (1 + r), and that total then compounds at (1 + r)<sup>12</sup> for every remaining year. The final value is the sum over all years, plus any lumpsum × (1 + r)<sup>months</sup>. The calculator does this month by month and shows the full schedule.

## स्टेप-अप SIP कैलकुलेटर (हिंदी में)

स्टेप-अप SIP में आप हर साल अपनी मासिक SIP बढ़ाते हैं, प्रतिशत से (जैसे 10%) या तय रकम से (जैसे ₹1,000)। ₹10,000 की SIP 20 साल तक, अनुमानित 12% रिटर्न पर, बिना बढ़ाए लगभग ₹1 करोड़ बनती है, और हर साल 10% बढ़ाने पर लगभग ₹1.99 करोड़। ये अनुमानित दरें हैं, गारंटी नहीं।

## Questions people ask

### What does a 10% step-up SIP mean?
Your monthly SIP rises by 10% every year: ₹10,000 in year one, ₹11,000 in year two, ₹12,100 in year three, and so on. Over 20 years at an assumed 12%, that grows to about ₹1.99 crore versus about ₹1 crore for a flat ₹10,000 SIP.

### Can I step up my SIP by a fixed amount instead of a percentage?
Yes. Choose "Fixed ₹ amount" above. Adding ₹1,000 a year to a ₹10,000 SIP for 20 years at an assumed 12% gives about ₹1.58 crore, with a year-20 SIP of ₹29,000.

### What is the step-up SIP formula?
Year k's monthly SIP is the first SIP × (1 + step-up %)^(k − 1), or the first SIP + (k − 1) × the fixed amount. Each instalment compounds monthly at the yearly return ÷ 12 until the end; the final value is the sum of all of them.

### How do I calculate a step-up SIP with inflation?
Divide the final value by (1 + inflation)^years. ₹1.99 crore after 20 years at 6% inflation is about ₹62 lakh in today's money. The calculator shows this as "In today's money".

### What is a good step-up percentage for a SIP?
Match it to your expected salary increase. For most salaried investors, 5 to 10% a year is sustainable. Pick the rate you can keep for the whole period, not the one that gives the biggest number.

### Can I set up a step-up SIP on an existing SIP?
Usually you register a new SIP with the top-up option, or add a separate SIP each year. Your distributor or the fund house app can set it up.

### Is a step-up SIP better than a normal SIP?
It builds a much bigger corpus from the same starting amount because you invest more over time. If you can already afford the higher amount, a bigger flat SIP from day one does even better.

### Does a step-up SIP cost anything extra?
No. There is no fee for the step-up feature. You simply invest more each year.

### Can I stop the step-up later?
Yes. You can cancel the top-up and continue at the current amount, or stop the SIP altogether.
