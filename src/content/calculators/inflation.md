|---|---|---|

| **SIP with inflation** | What will my SIP buy, after inflation? | ₹10,000 a month for 20 years |

Use **future cost** to price a goal, and **what money will be worth** to check whether a "₹1 crore at maturity" promise is as big as it sounds.

## The formulas

- Future cost = cost today × (1 + inflation)<sup>years</sup>
- Value in today's money = future amount ÷ (1 + inflation)<sup>years</sup>
- Old amount in today's rupees = old amount × (1 + average inflation)<sup>years since then</sup>
- Real (inflation-adjusted) return = (1 + return) ÷ (1 + inflation) − 1

A handy shortcut: divide 72 by the inflation rate to get the years in which prices double. At 6% that is 12 years. At 10% it is about 7.

## Which inflation rate should you use?

India's headline inflation is measured by the Consumer Price Index, and the RBI's target is 4%, within a band of 2 to 6%. Your own inflation can differ a lot from the headline because you buy a different basket. For planning:

| What you are pricing | Rate many planners use |
|---|---|
| Everyday household expenses | 5 to 6% |
| Retirement living costs | 6 to 7% |
| Higher education | 8 to 10% |
| Healthcare | 8 to 10% |

When unsure, use the higher number. A plan that over-saves by a little is far better than one that runs short.

## What is old money worth today?

To bring an amount from an earlier year into today's rupees, grow it by the average inflation since then.

| Old amount | Year | At an assumed 6% average, in 2026 rupees |
|---|---|---|
| ₹1 lakh | 2006 | ₹3.21 lakh |
| ₹10 lakh | 2014 | ₹20.1 lakh |
| ₹10 lakh | 2016 | ₹17.9 lakh |
| ₹1 lakh | 2020 | ₹1.42 lakh |

These use a steady assumed average. Actual inflation was different every year, so for an exact figure use the official Consumer Price Index (CPI) numbers published by the government. Choose **Old money in today's rupees** above to try your own year and rate.

## SIP with inflation: what will it really buy?

A SIP's maturity value is in future rupees. To see what it will buy, divide by inflation over the same years.

**Example:** ₹10,000 a month for 20 years at an assumed 12% return grows to about **₹99.9 lakh**. At 6% inflation, that buys what about **₹31.2 lakh** buys today. Your real return is about 5.7% a year: (1.12 ÷ 1.06) − 1. Assumed rates, not guaranteed.

That is why goals are best planned in future rupees, and why a yearly [step-up SIP](/calculators/step-up-sip-calculator) that grows with your income helps keep pace with rising prices.

## Why this matters for your savings

If your money earns less than inflation after tax, it is shrinking in real terms even though the balance goes up. A savings account at 3% while prices rise 6% loses about 3% of buying power a year. For goals more than five years away, part of the money usually needs to be in assets that have historically grown faster than inflation, such as equity mutual funds, accepting that their returns go up and down. The [goal SIP calculator](/calculators/goal-planner) builds inflation into the monthly SIP automatically.

## Questions people ask

### What is ₹10 lakh from 2014 worth today?
At an assumed average inflation of 6% a year, ₹10 lakh in 2014 is about ₹20.1 lakh in 2026 rupees. Choose "Old money in today's rupees" above to change the year or rate. For an exact figure, use official CPI data.

### What will ₹1 lakh be worth after 20 years of inflation?
At 6% inflation, ₹1 lakh in 20 years will buy what about ₹31,200 buys today. Something that costs ₹1 lakh today will cost about ₹3.21 lakh.

### How do I calculate an inflation-adjusted return?
Real return = (1 + return) ÷ (1 + inflation) − 1. A 12% return with 6% inflation is a real return of about 5.7% a year. The quick approximation, return minus inflation, gives 6%.

### How do I use a SIP calculator with inflation?

### What is the inflation rate in India?
India measures retail inflation with the Consumer Price Index (CPI). The RBI's target is 4%, within a band of 2% to 6%. For long-term goals, many people assume 5 to 6% for general expenses and more for education and healthcare.

### What will ₹1 lakh be worth in 10 years?
At 6% inflation, ₹1 lakh in 10 years will buy what about ₹55,800 buys today. Put the other way, what costs ₹1 lakh today will cost about ₹1.79 lakh.

### What inflation rate should I assume for goal planning in India?
5 to 6% for general expenses, and 8 to 10% for education and healthcare, which have tended to rise faster. Use the higher end when in doubt.

### How do I calculate the future value of money with inflation?
Divide the future amount by (1 + inflation rate) raised to the number of years. The calculator does this when you choose "What money will be worth".

### How long does it take for prices to double?
Roughly 72 divided by the inflation rate: about 12 years at 6%, 9 years at 8% and 7 years at 10%.

### Does inflation affect my SIP returns?
Yes. The real return is roughly the return minus inflation. A 12% return with 6% inflation grows your buying power by a little under 6% a year (about 5.7%).
