## How to use the SIP calculator

1. Pick **Monthly SIP** at the top. The same tool also does a [step-up SIP](/calculators/step-up-sip-calculator), a one-time [lumpsum](/calculators/lumpsum-calculator), and SIP plus lumpsum together.
2. Enter your **monthly amount**. You can type "15k" or "1 lakh".
3. Set the **expected return** and the **number of years**.

You get three numbers: what you put in, what it could grow to, and the gain. The chart shows the gap between the two lines widening over time, which is compounding doing its work.

## The SIP formula

**Future value = P × [((1 + r)<sup>n</sup> − 1) ÷ r] × (1 + r)**

- *P* is the monthly SIP
- *r* is the monthly return, the yearly return divided by 12
- *n* is the number of monthly instalments

Each instalment is assumed to go in at the start of the month, which is how most fund house calculators work too.

## How sensitive is the answer to the return?

Very. The same ₹10,000 for 20 years is about ₹76.6 lakh at 10% and ₹99.9 lakh at 12%. Two percentage points, held for two decades, change the result by almost a quarter. So:

- run the calculator at a lower return as well as your hopeful one, and plan on the lower one
- remember that a plan's costs (the expense ratio) come out of the return, so they matter more over long periods

## SIP returns are measured with XIRR, not CAGR

Because each instalment is invested on a different date, the right way to measure the return on an actual SIP is XIRR. Your account statement shows it (see [XIRR in the glossary](/mutual-fund-glossary#term-xirr)). Use the [CAGR calculator](/calculators/cagr-calculator) only for a single, one-time investment.

## Questions people ask

### How much will ₹5,000 a month in SIP become in 20 years?
About ₹50 lakh at an assumed 12% yearly return. You would invest ₹12 lakh of that yourself.

### How does a SIP grow over time?
Each instalment buys units at that day's NAV and compounds from then on. Early instalments compound the longest, which is why time in the market matters more than the exact start date.

### Is the SIP return guaranteed?
No. Mutual fund returns depend on the market and can be negative in some years. The calculator shows what happens at a steady return you choose; real returns arrive unevenly.

### What is the minimum amount to start a SIP?
Many schemes accept SIPs from ₹500 a month, and some from ₹100. You can increase it later or add a yearly step-up.

### Can I stop or pause a SIP?
Yes. Open-ended fund SIPs can be paused or stopped at any time without penalty. Units you already hold stay invested. ELSS units are locked for three years from each instalment.
