## A fair comparison, not a sales pitch

Many "prepay vs invest" calculators compare the interest you save with the wealth a SIP builds, and the SIP always wins. That comparison is rigged: it forgets that once you prepay and close the loan early, your whole EMI is free to invest for the remaining years.

This calculator compares two plans that spend **exactly the same money every month** and are **debt-free on the same date**:

- **Plan A, prepay first.** Pay the EMI plus your surplus into the loan until it closes, then put that whole amount into a SIP until the original loan end date.
- **Plan B, invest from day one.** Pay the normal EMI and put the surplus into a SIP straight away.

Whichever has more money at the end is the better plan for your numbers.

## How to use it

1. **Outstanding loan**, **interest rate** and **remaining tenure**.
2. **Extra monthly surplus**: the amount you could use to prepay or to invest.
3. **Expected SIP return**.

## Worked example

₹45 lakh outstanding at 8.75% for 15 years; EMI ₹44,975; surplus ₹15,000 a month.

| | Plan A: prepay, then SIP | Plan B: SIP from day one |
|---|---|---|
| Loan closes | in 9.2 years | in 15 years |
| Interest saved | ₹15.6 lakh | — |
| Wealth after 15 years at 13% | ₹64.3 lakh | ₹83.4 lakh |
| at 12% | ₹62.2 lakh | ₹75.7 lakh |
| at 10% | ₹58.2 lakh | ₹62.7 lakh |
| at 8.75% | ₹55.9 lakh | ₹55.9 lakh |

The lead shrinks as the assumed return falls and disappears at the loan rate. Below that, prepaying wins.

## What the maths leaves out

- **Tax on SIP gains.** Long-term equity gains above ₹1.25 lakh a year are taxed at 12.5%, which trims Plan B's lead.
- **Tax on loan interest.** Under the old tax regime, up to ₹2 lakh a year of interest on a self-occupied home loan is deductible, which lowers your effective loan rate. The new regime does not allow it.
- **Risk.** The 8.75% you save by prepaying is certain. Equity returns are not, and a bad decade can happen.
- **Peace of mind.** Being debt-free has a value no spreadsheet captures, especially near retirement or with a single income.

## A practical middle path

Many families split the surplus: part to prepay, part to a SIP. Some invest first and then use the SIP to close the loan when the fund value passes the outstanding balance. Whatever you choose, clear credit cards and personal loans before either; at 12 to 40% interest, prepaying them beats any investment.

## Questions people ask

### Should I prepay my home loan or invest in a SIP?
If your SIP earns more than your loan rate over the period, investing leaves you with more money. If not, prepaying does. Prepaying is a certain return equal to your loan rate; SIP returns are not guaranteed. Many people split their surplus between the two.

### Is it better to reduce EMI or tenure when prepaying?
Reducing the tenure saves more interest. Reducing the EMI frees up monthly cash flow. If the goal is to save interest, keep the EMI and cut the tenure.

### Are there charges for prepaying a home loan?
Banks and housing finance companies do not charge prepayment penalties on floating-rate home loans taken by individuals. Fixed-rate loans may carry a charge; check your loan agreement.

### At what interest rate should I prepay instead of investing?
When your loan rate is close to or above the return you can reasonably expect after tax, prepaying usually makes more sense. Personal loans and credit cards should almost always be prepaid first.

### Can I use my SIP to close the home loan later?
Yes. Once the SIP value exceeds the remaining loan, you can redeem to close it. Remember that redeeming may trigger capital gains tax, and the timing depends on actual returns.
