## How to use the emergency fund calculator

1. Enter your **essential monthly outflows**: rent or home loan EMI, groceries and bills, school fees and medical costs, and insurance premiums. Leave out holidays, shopping and investments; in an emergency those stop.
2. Enter what you already have **in the bank or in liquid funds** that you could use in a day or two.

The calculator shows three levels (3, 6 and 12 months of expenses), your shortfall against six months, and a suggested way to split the money.

## How much is enough?

| Your situation | Months of essential expenses |
|---|---|
| Two stable incomes, no dependants | 3 to 6 |
| One salaried income, or a family to support | 6 |
| Self-employed, business owner, or commission-based income | 9 to 12 |
| Close to retirement, or with a health condition in the family | 12 |

Health insurance changes the picture. Without a family floater policy, one hospital stay can use up the whole fund. Buy cover first, then build the fund.

## Worked example

| Item | Monthly |
|---|---|
| Rent or EMI | ₹30,000 |
| Groceries, electricity, bills and fuel | ₹20,000 |
| School fees and medical | ₹15,000 |
| Insurance premiums | ₹5,000 |
| **Total essentials** | **₹70,000** |

Six months is ₹4.2 lakh. With ₹1.5 lakh already saved, the shortfall is ₹2.7 lakh. Saving ₹22,500 a month closes that in a year.

## Where to keep it

An emergency fund has one job: be there, in full, when you need it. Return comes last.

- **Savings account**: a month or so of expenses, for instant access.
- **Overnight or liquid mutual funds**: most of the rest. They invest in very short-term debt, have historically had low volatility, and redemptions usually reach your bank on the next working day. Many liquid funds also offer instant redemption of up to ₹50,000.
- **Sweep-in FD** linked to your savings account: another good option.

Keep emergency money out of equity funds, ELSS, PPF and anything with a lock-in or exit load.

## Questions people ask

### How much emergency fund should I keep?
Six months of essential expenses is a common starting point; twelve months if you have a single income, are self-employed or have dependants.

### Where should I keep an emergency fund?
Somewhere safe and quick to access, such as a savings account, a sweep-in fixed deposit or a liquid mutual fund that can be redeemed within a working day.

### Should I build an emergency fund before starting a SIP?
Ideally build at least three months first, then run both: keep adding to the emergency fund while a SIP starts for long-term goals.

### Is a liquid fund safe for an emergency fund?
Liquid funds invest in very short-term, high-quality debt and have historically been among the least volatile mutual funds. They are not guaranteed like a bank deposit, so choose a large fund with good-quality holdings.

### Does a credit card count as an emergency fund?
No. A credit card can bridge a few days, but its interest is very high. Use it only until you can withdraw from your real emergency fund.
