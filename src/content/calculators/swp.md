## How to use the SWP calculator

1. **Total corpus**: the amount you will invest and draw from.
2. **Monthly payout**: what you want to receive each month.
3. **Yearly increase in payout**: set this to your expected inflation (say 5 to 6%) if you want your income to keep its buying power. Leave it at 0 for a flat payout.
4. **Expected return** and **withdrawal period**.

The schedule shows, year by year, what you withdrew, what the remaining money earned, and the closing balance. If the balance hits zero, the calculator tells you the year.

## Worked example: ₹50 lakh corpus

| Monthly payout | Rises each year by | Return | What happens |
|---|---|---|---|
| ₹25,000 | 0% | 8% | Never runs out; about ₹99 lakh left after 20 years |
| ₹35,000 | 0% | 8.5% | ₹84 lakh paid out over 20 years, ₹52.6 lakh still left |
| ₹40,000 | 0% | 8% | Runs out in about 22 years |
| ₹25,000 | 6% | 8% | Runs out in about 21 years |
| ₹25,000 | 6% | 10% | Lasts about 30 years |

The fourth row is the one most people miss. A flat ₹25,000 looks safe, but you will need more every year. Once the payout rises with inflation, the same corpus lasts about two decades rather than forever.

## A simple rule for a sustainable SWP

If your yearly withdrawal is **less than the return the corpus earns**, the corpus keeps growing. ₹25,000 a month is ₹3 lakh a year, which is 6% of ₹50 lakh, below an 8% return. When you plan for rising withdrawals, start lower: around 4 to 5% of the corpus in the first year is a common cautious starting point.

## How SWP income is taxed

Each withdrawal is treated as selling some units. Only the **gain** in those units is taxed, not the whole payout. In the early years most of each withdrawal is your own capital coming back, so the tax is small.

- **Equity funds** held over 12 months: 12.5% on gains above ₹1.25 lakh a year. Held 12 months or less: 20%.
- **Debt funds** bought on or after 1 April 2023: gains taxed at your slab rate.

This often makes an SWP more tax-efficient than interest from an FD, where the full interest is taxable. The [mutual fund tax calculator](/calculators/mutual-fund-tax-calculator) works out the tax on any redemption.

## Setting it up well

- Keep two to three years of withdrawals in a liquid or short-duration debt fund, and refill it from equity in good years. That way a market fall never forces you to sell equity at a low.
- Start the SWP after any exit-load period is over. Many equity funds charge about 1% on units redeemed within a year.
- Review the payout once a year.

## Questions people ask

### What is a systematic withdrawal plan (SWP)?
An SWP pays you a fixed amount from your mutual fund every month or quarter. The rest of the money stays invested, which can help the corpus last longer.

### How is SWP income taxed?
Only the capital gain portion of each withdrawal is taxed, not the full amount. Tax depends on the fund type and how long the units were held.

### How much monthly income can I get from ₹1 crore?
If you want the corpus to last 25 to 30 years with the income rising with inflation, a starting withdrawal of around ₹35,000 to ₹40,000 a month (4 to 5% a year) is a cautious figure. A higher payout is possible if you accept a shorter period.

### Is SWP better than an FD for monthly income?
An SWP from a balanced or debt-oriented fund can be more tax-efficient and can grow, but its value moves with the market. An FD's interest is fixed but fully taxable. Many retirees use both.

### Which funds are used for an SWP?
Retirees often use balanced advantage, conservative hybrid or short-duration debt funds, sometimes with a separate equity fund for growth. The mix should depend on how long the money must last.
