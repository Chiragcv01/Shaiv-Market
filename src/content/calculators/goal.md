## How to use the goal SIP calculator

1. **Goal cost in today's money**: what the goal would cost if you bought it today. A four-year engineering degree, a home down payment, a wedding budget.
2. **Years to the goal**.
3. **Inflation for this goal**: general prices often rise 5 to 6% a year, while education and medical costs have tended to rise faster, so many planners use 8 to 10% for them.
4. **Savings already set aside** for this goal, and the **expected return**.

The planner gives the future cost, how much of it your existing savings will cover, the monthly SIP for the rest, and the one-time amount that would do the same job today.

## How the maths works

1. Future cost = today's cost × (1 + goal inflation)<sup>years</sup>
2. Existing savings grow at your expected return to the goal date.
3. The shortfall is converted into a monthly SIP with the same formula as the [target SIP calculator](/calculators/required-sip-calculator).

## Worked example: a child's higher education

Your daughter is 3. A good degree costs ₹20 lakh today and she will start college in 15 years.

| Step | Figure |
|---|---|
| Cost today | ₹20 lakh |
| Cost in 15 years at 10% education inflation | ₹83.5 lakh |
| Monthly SIP needed at 12% | ₹16,557 |
| Or start at, with a 10% yearly step-up | about ₹9,600 |

If you start when she is 8 instead, with 10 years to go, the cost is about ₹51.9 lakh and the SIP is ₹22,327 a month. Waiting five years makes the goal cheaper in rupees but the SIP 35% bigger.

## Plan each goal separately

It is tempting to run one big SIP "for everything". Separate goals work better because each one gets the right fund mix for its timeline. A goal 15 years away can sit mostly in equity. A goal 2 years away should already be moving into debt or liquid funds so that a market fall in the final year can't derail it.

For a goal with a fixed date, start shifting from equity to safer funds two to three years before you need the money.

## Questions people ask

### How do I plan for my child's higher education?
Estimate today's cost, inflate it at 8 to 10% a year to the year of admission, and work out the monthly SIP needed. Move gradually to safer funds in the last two or three years.

### Can I plan several goals at once?
Yes. Plan each goal separately with its own amount, date and fund mix, so a near goal is not exposed to the same risk as a far one.

### What inflation rate should I use for a goal?
5 to 6% for general expenses, 8 to 10% for education and healthcare, and the actual price trend for things like property in your city. When unsure, use the higher figure.

### What if I already have some money saved for the goal?
Enter it in the savings box. The planner grows it to the goal date and reduces the SIP needed accordingly.

### Should I use a child plan from an insurance company instead?
Insurance and investment usually work better separately: a term plan for protection, and a SIP for the goal. Compare the charges and lock-in before you buy any combined product.
