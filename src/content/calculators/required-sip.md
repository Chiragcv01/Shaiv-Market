## How to use the target SIP calculator

1. **Target amount.** Type the amount you want to have, for example 1 crore or 50 lakh. If your goal is priced in today's money, inflate it first with the [inflation calculator](/calculators/inflation-calculator).
2. **Time period.** The number of years until you need the money.
3. **Expected return.** The yearly return you assume. For diversified equity funds over long periods, 10 to 12% is a common planning range.
4. **Step-up (optional).** If you will raise the SIP every year with your salary, add the percentage. The starting SIP drops sharply.

The result is the monthly SIP to start now, the total you will put in, and a year-by-year schedule showing how close you are to the target each year.

## The formula behind it

This is the SIP future value formula turned around:

**Monthly SIP = Target × r ÷ [((1 + r)<sup>n</sup> − 1) × (1 + r)]**

where *r* is the monthly return (yearly return ÷ 12) and *n* is the number of months. The extra (1 + r) is there because each instalment is invested at the start of the month.

## Worked example: ₹1 crore

| Years | SIP at 12% | SIP at 10% | At 12% with a 10% yearly step-up |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10 | ₹43,041 | ₹48,414 | ₹29,636 to start |
| 15 | ₹19,819 | ₹23,928 | ₹11,516 to start |
| 20 | ₹10,009 | ₹13,060 | ₹5,028 to start |
| 25 | ₹5,270 | ₹7,474 | ₹2,339 to start |

Two things stand out. Time does most of the work: going from 15 to 20 years roughly halves the SIP. And a step-up turns an intimidating number into a starting amount most salaried people can manage.

## Don't forget inflation

₹1 crore in 15 years will not buy what ₹1 crore buys today. At 6% inflation, a goal worth ₹50 lakh today will cost about ₹1.20 crore in 15 years. The SIP for that, at 12%, is about ₹23,748 a month, not the ₹9,900 you would get by planning for ₹50 lakh. Always plan in future rupees. The [goal planner](/calculators/goal-planner) does the inflation step for you.

## What to do with the number

If the SIP is more than you can manage today, you have four levers: start smaller with a step-up, push the goal out by a few years, add a lumpsum you already have, or accept a smaller target. Changing the return assumption is not a lever. Choosing a riskier fund to "need" a smaller SIP is how plans go wrong.

## Questions people ask

### How much SIP is needed for ₹1 crore in 15 years?
About ₹19,800 a month at an assumed 12% yearly return. At 10% it rises to roughly ₹23,900. With a 10% yearly step-up at 12%, you can start at about ₹11,500 a month.

### How much SIP for ₹50 lakh in 10 years?
About ₹21,500 a month at 12%, or ₹24,200 at 10%. Use the calculator above for any other combination.

### Should I plan for inflation?
Yes. A goal that costs ₹25 lakh today can cost about ₹50 lakh in 12 years at 6% inflation. Set the target in future rupees, or raise your SIP every year with a step-up.

### Is it better to increase the SIP or the time period?
Time is the stronger lever, because the money you put in early compounds the longest. If the date is fixed, a yearly step-up is the next best tool.

### What if markets do worse than I assumed?
Then you will fall short unless you adjust. That is why the plan should be reviewed once a year: if returns lag, raise the SIP a little early rather than a lot late.
