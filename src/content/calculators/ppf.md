## How to use the PPF calculator

1. **Yearly deposit**: anything from ₹500 to ₹1,50,000 in a financial year.
2. **Interest rate**: pre-filled with 7.1%, the rate for October to December 2026. The government reviews it every quarter, so you can change it.
3. **Account period**: 15 years, or longer if you plan to extend in 5-year blocks.

## How PPF interest is calculated

PPF interest is worked out every month on the **lowest balance between the 5th and the last day of the month**, and added to your account once a year, at the end of March. That is why it pays to deposit before the 5th, and best of all before **5 April**, so the whole year's deposit earns interest for all twelve months.

The calculator assumes exactly that: the full deposit at the start of each year, compounding yearly.

**Maturity value = yearly deposit × [((1 + r)<sup>n</sup> − 1) ÷ r] × (1 + r)**

## Worked example: ₹1.5 lakh a year at 7.1%

| Period | Your deposits | Interest earned | Maturity value |
|---|---|---|---|
| 15 years | ₹22.5 lakh | ₹18.2 lakh | ₹40.7 lakh |
| 20 years | ₹30.0 lakh | ₹36.6 lakh | ₹66.6 lakh |
| 25 years | ₹37.5 lakh | ₹65.6 lakh | ₹1.03 crore |
| 30 years | ₹45.0 lakh | ₹1.10 crore | ₹1.55 crore |

The extensions are where PPF really shines: in years 16 to 30 the interest earned is far larger than in the first 15, because it is calculated on a much bigger balance.

## The rules worth knowing

- **Lock-in**: 15 financial years. After that you can close the account or extend it in blocks of 5 years, with or without fresh deposits.
- **Loans** are available from the 3rd to the 5th financial year, up to 25% of an earlier balance.
- **Partial withdrawals** are allowed from the 7th financial year, up to 50% of an earlier balance.
- **Premature closure** after 5 years is allowed only for specific reasons such as serious illness or higher education, with a 1% interest penalty.
- **Tax**: interest and maturity are tax-free. Under the old tax regime, deposits also qualify for the deduction that used to be Section 80C (now Section 123 of the Income-tax Act, 2025) within the ₹1.5 lakh limit. The new regime does not give this deduction.

## PPF or an equity SIP?

They do different jobs. PPF gives a government-backed, tax-free return with no market risk, but a long lock-in. An equity SIP has no lock-in (except ELSS) and has the potential for higher long-term growth, but its value goes up and down.

For comparison only: ₹12,500 a month in a SIP for 15 years would be about ₹63 lakh at an assumed 12%, or about ₹52 lakh at 10%, against PPF's ₹40.7 lakh. That higher figure is not guaranteed and is taxable. Many families use PPF as the safe anchor for long goals and SIPs for growth. The [ELSS guide](/blog/elss-tax-saving-guide) compares the tax-saving options.

## Questions people ask

### What is the PPF interest rate now?
7.1% a year for October to December 2026. The Ministry of Finance announces the rate every quarter.

### How much will I get if I invest ₹1.5 lakh a year in PPF for 15 years?
About ₹40.7 lakh at 7.1%, if you deposit by 5 April every year. You put in ₹22.5 lakh; the remaining ₹18.2 lakh is tax-free interest.

### Can I deposit monthly in PPF instead of once a year?
Yes. You can deposit in instalments during the year. Deposits made after the 5th of a month earn no interest for that month, so a single deposit before 5 April earns the most.

### Can I withdraw from PPF before 15 years?
Partly. Partial withdrawals are allowed from the 7th financial year, and the account can be closed early after 5 years only for reasons such as illness or higher education, with a small interest penalty.

### What happens to PPF after 15 years?
You can withdraw the full amount, or extend the account in blocks of 5 years. If you do nothing, it continues without fresh deposits and keeps earning interest. To keep depositing, you must apply for the extension within a year of maturity.
