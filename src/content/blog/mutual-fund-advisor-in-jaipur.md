Most people who type "mutual fund advisor in Jaipur" into Google want one simple thing: someone who knows what they are doing to look at their money and tell them what to do next.

The catch is that in India "adviser" is a regulated word. Before you pick someone, it helps to know the two kinds of professionals who show up in that search, because they are paid differently and they are allowed to do different things.

I'll be upfront: I am not an adviser. I am an AMFI registered mutual fund distributor (ARN-337289). I wrote this so you can choose with your eyes open, even if the right choice for you is someone else.

## The two kinds of "mutual fund advisor"

| | SEBI registered investment adviser (RIA) | Mutual fund distributor (MFD) |
|---|---|---|
| Registered with | SEBI | AMFI (with an ARN) |
| Exam | NISM Series X-A and X-B (investment adviser) | NISM Series V-A |
| How they are paid | A fee you pay directly | Commission from the fund house |
| Which plans | Direct plans | Regular plans |
| Can use the word "adviser" | Yes | No |
| Handles your transactions | Usually not, you execute | Yes, end to end |

## The RIA route

An RIA is like a doctor who writes the prescription but doesn't run the pharmacy. You pay a fee, either a fixed amount or a small percentage of what they advise on, within limits SEBI sets. In return they owe you advice that is in your interest and free of product commissions.

You'll usually invest in direct plans yourself, through the fund house website, MF Central or an app. That keeps your costs low, but you do the paperwork and you hold your own nerve when markets fall.

An RIA makes the most sense if you have a larger portfolio, want a full financial plan (insurance, loans, tax, estate), and are comfortable executing on your own.

You can check whether someone is a registered adviser on SEBI's website under its list of registered intermediaries.

## The MFD route

A distributor helps you choose suitable schemes, completes KYC, sets up SIPs, tracks everything, and reviews it with you. There is no bill. The distributor earns a trail commission from the fund house, which is built into the regular plan's expense ratio.

That cost is real and I don't hide it. I've put numbers on it in [direct vs regular mutual funds](/blog/direct-vs-regular-mutual-fund). What you get for it is a person who does the work and stays with you through the boring years and the scary ones.

An MFD makes most sense if you are starting out, if your time is better spent on your work or business, or if you know you might stop a SIP at the wrong moment without someone to talk to.

## Why the word matters

SEBI has told distributors not to use names or titles like "adviser", "IFA" (independent financial adviser) or "wealth manager" unless they are registered for that role. The point is to stop investors from thinking they are getting paid, conflict-free advice when they are not.

So if you meet a "financial advisor" in Jaipur, ask one question: **"Are you a SEBI registered investment adviser, or an AMFI registered distributor?"** Either answer is fine. A vague answer is not.

## Which one should you pick?

- **Starting with ₹2,000 to ₹25,000 a month in SIPs?** A distributor is usually the practical choice. Getting started and staying invested matters more than the cost difference at this stage.
- **Portfolio in crores, complex taxes, a business to plan around?** Consider a fee-only RIA, possibly alongside your own execution.

## Questions people ask

### Can a mutual fund distributor call himself a financial advisor?
No. SEBI has said distributors should not use terms such as adviser, IFA or wealth manager in their names or communication unless they hold the matching registration. A distributor should clearly say they are an AMFI registered mutual fund distributor.

### Who is cheaper, an RIA or an MFD?
An RIA charges you a visible fee but you invest in lower-cost direct plans. An MFD charges no fee but you invest in regular plans with a higher expense ratio. Which works out cheaper depends on your portfolio size and how much help you need.

### How do I find a SEBI registered investment adviser in Jaipur?
Check SEBI's website for the list of registered investment advisers and confirm the registration number the person gives you.
