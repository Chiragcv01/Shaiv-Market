When a child is three, college feels like a long way off. That's the best news a parent can have, because time is the one thing that makes this goal affordable.

## Step 1: Price the course in future rupees

Education costs in India have risen faster than general inflation for years. Planning at **8% to 10% a year** is sensible; we'll use 10%.

A course that costs **₹20 lakh today** (a private engineering degree, a good MBA, or a year abroad) becomes:

- **₹51.9 lakh** in 10 years
- **₹83.5 lakh** in 15 years

Yes, really. That's what 10% a year does over 15 years.

## Step 2: Find the monthly SIP

At an assumed 12% yearly return:

| Child's age today | Years left | Future cost | Flat monthly SIP | Or start at (10% step-up) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | 15 | ₹83.5 lakh | ₹16,600 | ₹9,600 |
| 8 | 10 | ₹51.9 lakh | ₹22,300 | ₹15,400 |

Two things jump out:

1. **The step-up makes it manageable.** Starting at ₹9,600 and raising it 10% a year, in line with your income, gets you there.
2. **Waiting five years costs more every month, for a smaller target.** At age 8 you pay ₹22,300 a month. At age 3 it's ₹16,600, even though the goal is bigger.

## Step 3: Protect the money near the finish line

The worst thing that can happen is a 30% market fall the year before admission. So the plan has two phases:

- **Until about 3 years before the goal:** mostly diversified equity funds for growth.
- **Final 3 years:** move the money gradually, a slice every few months, into short-duration debt or hybrid funds. By admission year, most of it should be safe and available.

This "glide path" is the single most important part of a child education plan, and the part most people forget.

## A few practical points

- **Keep it separate.** Have a dedicated folio, or at least a dedicated fund, for this goal so it doesn't get used for a car or a holiday.
- **Nominee and guardian details:** make sure every folio has a nominee. If you invest in the child's name, a parent acts as guardian until the child turns 18.
- **More than one child?** Plan each child's goal separately; their timelines are different.
- **Don't skip life insurance.** A plain term plan protects the goal if something happens to the earning parent. The SIP builds the money; the term plan makes sure the plan survives you.

## Questions people ask

### What is the best investment for a child's education in India?
For goals 10 or more years away, diversified equity mutual fund SIPs have historically offered the best chance of beating education inflation, combined with a gradual shift to debt funds in the last three years. Sukanya Samriddhi suits parents of girls who want a government-backed option.

### How much should I save for my child's higher education?
Estimate today's cost of the course, increase it at about 8% to 10% a year until the admission year, and use a SIP calculator to find the monthly amount. For a ₹20 lakh course 15 years away, that's about ₹16,600 a month at an assumed 12% return.

### Should I invest in my child's name or my own name?
Either works. Investing in the child's name keeps the money clearly earmarked, with a parent as guardian until 18. Investing in your own name gives you more flexibility. Income from investments in a minor child's name is usually clubbed with the parent's income for tax.
