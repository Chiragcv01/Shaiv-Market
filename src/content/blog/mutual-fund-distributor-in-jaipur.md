## What a mutual fund distributor actually does

A mutual fund distributor, often shortened to MFD, is registered with AMFI (the Association of Mutual Funds in India) and holds an ARN, the AMFI Registration Number. To get it, the person has to pass the NISM Series V-A certification exam and renew it every three years.

In practice, a distributor:

- helps you pick schemes that fit a goal, a time frame and your comfort with ups and downs
- completes your KYC and opens your folios, usually on your phone in a few minutes
- sets up SIPs, switches and redemptions, and tracks them so nothing gets missed
- sends you a consolidated view of everything you hold, across fund houses
- sits with you once a year (or when life changes) to rebalance

That last point is the part people underrate. The real value of a distributor is rarely the fund selection. It is the call in a bad month that stops you from selling at the bottom.

## What it costs you

You do not write a cheque to a mutual fund distributor. When you invest through a distributor, you invest in the **regular plan** of a scheme. The fund house pays the distributor a trail commission, and that cost is part of the regular plan's expense ratio.

A **direct plan** of the same scheme has no distributor commission, so its expense ratio is lower. Over 20 years that difference adds up to real money, and I have written a separate, honest comparison: [Direct vs regular mutual funds](/blog/direct-vs-regular-mutual-fund).

So the right question is not "is a distributor free?" It is "is the help worth the cost of the regular plan for me?" For someone who would otherwise never start, or who panics in a falling market, it usually is. For a disciplined do-it-yourself investor, a direct plan may suit better.

## Seven questions to ask any distributor in Jaipur

1. **What is your ARN, and can I see it on the AMFI website?** Anyone serious will tell you without hesitation. Here is [how to verify an ARN in two minutes](/blog/verify-mutual-fund-distributor-arn).
2. **How are you paid?** You want a straight answer: commission from the fund house through regular plans. Be careful if someone calls the service "completely free" without explaining this.
3. **Do you call yourself an advisor?** Under SEBI rules, a distributor cannot present themselves as an "adviser", "IFA" or "wealth manager". If the business card says "financial advisor" but there is no SEBI investment adviser registration, that tells you how the rest will go.
4. **Where does my money go?** It should go only from your bank account to the mutual fund's own account (through BSE StAR MF, NSE or the fund house). Never to a personal or company account of the distributor.
5. **How will you choose funds for me?** Look for an answer about your goals, time frame and risk, not about last year's top returns.
6. **What happens after I invest?** Ask how often they review, how you will get statements, and what they do when markets fall.
7. **Can I meet you?** For a lot of families in Jaipur, sitting across the table still matters. A distributor with a fixed office address is easier to hold accountable.

## Red flags

- Guaranteed or "fixed" returns on an equity fund. No one can promise this.
- Pressure to invest today because an NFO (new fund offer) closes this week.
- Frequent switching between funds without a clear reason (each switch can trigger tax and exit load).
- Asking for your net banking password, OTPs or a signed blank form.
- No ARN, an expired ARN, or a name on the website that doesn't match the AMFI record.

## Questions people ask

### Is a mutual fund distributor the same as a mutual fund agent?
Yes. "Agent" is the older, informal word. The official term is mutual fund distributor (MFD), and the person must hold a valid ARN from AMFI.

### Do I pay a fee to a mutual fund distributor in Jaipur?
No separate fee. The distributor is paid a commission by the fund house, which is included in the expense ratio of the regular plan you invest in.

### Can a mutual fund distributor give investment advice?
A distributor can explain products and help you choose suitable schemes as part of distribution. Formal, fee-based investment advice is the job of a SEBI registered investment adviser (RIA). Read [mutual fund advisor in Jaipur: RIA or MFD?](/blog/mutual-fund-advisor-in-jaipur) for the difference.
