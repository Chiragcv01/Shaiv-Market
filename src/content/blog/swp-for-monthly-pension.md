Retirement is the moment your salary stops but your bills don't. A systematic withdrawal plan, or SWP, is one of the cleanest ways to turn a lump sum into a monthly income without locking it away.

## How an SWP works

You invest a lump sum, say your PF and gratuity, in one or more mutual funds. Then you instruct the fund house to pay a fixed amount to your bank account every month.

Each month, the fund sells just enough units to pay you. Whatever isn't withdrawn keeps earning. Think of it as a pension you control: you can raise it, lower it, pause it or take out a lump sum whenever you need.

## How long will ₹50 lakh last?

Assume ₹50 lakh earns a steady 8% or 10% a year. (Real returns won't be steady, and we'll come back to that.)

**If you withdraw the same amount every month:**

| Monthly withdrawal | At 8% a year | At 10% a year |
|---|---|---|
| ₹25,000 | Lasts 50+ years | Lasts 50+ years |
| ₹30,000 | Lasts 50+ years | Lasts 50+ years |
| ₹40,000 | About 22 years | Lasts 50+ years |

Looks comfortable. Here is the catch.

## The catch: your expenses will rise

₹25,000 today will not buy the same groceries, medicines and electricity in 15 years. If you raise your withdrawal by **6% a year** to keep up with inflation:

| Starting withdrawal | At 8% a year | At 10% a year |
|---|---|---|
| ₹25,000, rising 6% a year | About 21 years | About 30 years |

That's the real planning number. If you retire at 60, 21 years takes you to 81, which may not be enough. Retiring with a bigger corpus, starting with a smaller withdrawal, or keeping part of the money in equity for growth all extend it.

## Where to keep the money

A common approach is to split the corpus into buckets:

- **Next 2 to 3 years of withdrawals:** liquid or short-duration debt funds, so a market fall never forces you to sell equity at a loss.
- **Years 3 to 8:** hybrid or conservative funds.
- **The rest:** a diversified equity fund to keep growing and beat inflation.

You withdraw from the first bucket and refill it from the others once a year, ideally after a good year in markets.

## How SWP income is taxed

This is where an SWP shines over FD interest. Each withdrawal is partly your own money coming back and partly gain. **Only the gain portion is taxed**, not the whole amount.

- From equity funds held over 12 months: 12.5% on long-term gains above ₹1.25 lakh a year.
- From debt funds bought after 1 April 2023: gains are added to your income and taxed at your slab rate.

In the early years most of each withdrawal is your own capital, so the tax is usually small.

## SWP vs FD interest vs annuity

- **FD interest:** fixed, simple, but fully taxed every year at your slab, and the capital doesn't grow.
- **Annuity:** guaranteed for life, but the rate is fixed at purchase and you usually can't get the money back.
- **SWP:** flexible and tax-efficient, but it depends on markets, so it needs a sensible mix and a yearly check-up.

Many retirees use two of the three together.

## Questions people ask

### What is a safe withdrawal rate for SWP in India?
Withdrawing about 4% to 5% of the corpus in the first year and raising it with inflation is a reasonable starting point for long retirements. Higher rates can work for shorter time frames but increase the risk of running out.

### Is SWP better than FD for monthly income?
SWP is usually more tax-efficient because only the gain portion of each withdrawal is taxed, and part of the money can stay in growth assets. An FD gives certainty. Many retirees use both.

### Can I stop or change an SWP?
Yes. You can raise, lower, pause or stop an SWP at any time by instructing the fund house or your distributor. There is no lock-in, although exit loads may apply on recent investments.
